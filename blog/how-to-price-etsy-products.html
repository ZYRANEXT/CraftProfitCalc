How to Price Etsy Products Without Losing Money

You made a sale. The notification felt exciting. Someone found your product, liked it enough to buy it, and trusted your shop with their money.

Then the numbers started showing up.

Materials. Packaging. Shipping. Etsy fees. Payment processing. Maybe a discount code. Maybe an ad fee. Suddenly, the sale that looked profitable starts feeling much smaller.

This is one of the most common problems for Etsy sellers: the shop is getting orders, but the money is not staying in the business. The issue is not always the product. Sometimes the issue is the price.

This guide will show you how to price Etsy products without guessing, without copying competitors blindly, and without accidentally selling products that make almost no profit. You will also learn how to use CraftProfitCalc to check your numbers before you list your next product.

The hidden problem most Etsy sellers miss

The biggest pricing mistake is confusing revenue with profit.

If you sell a product for $30, you did not make $30. You generated $30 in sales revenue. Profit is what remains after every cost and fee is removed.

Revenue is the money that comes in. Profit is the money that stays.

That difference matters because Etsy selling has several layers of cost. Some are obvious, like materials. Others are easier to forget, like packaging, labels, payment processing, replacement orders, and ads.

Many sellers look at a competitor selling something similar for $18 and assume they need to match that price. But you do not know that competitor's material cost, labor time, supplier discounts, shipping arrangement, ad strategy, or profit goals. Copying a price without knowing the math can push your shop into a low-margin trap.

The simple Etsy pricing formula every seller should know

Profit = Selling Price + Shipping Charged - Etsy Fees - Product Cost - Shipping Cost - Packaging Cost - Ad Cost

This formula is simple, but it forces you to look at the full picture.

  • Selling Price: the price of the item itself.
  • Shipping Charged: what the customer pays for shipping, if anything.
  • Etsy Fees: listing fees, transaction fees, payment processing fees, and optional ad-related fees.
  • Product Cost: materials, supplies, printing, production, or wholesale cost.
  • Shipping Cost: what you actually pay to ship the order.
  • Packaging Cost: boxes, mailers, tissue, labels, stickers, inserts, and protective material.
  • Ad Cost: Etsy Ads, Offsite Ads, or any promotion cost tied to the sale.

The goal is not to make the formula complicated. The goal is to stop forgetting the small costs that quietly destroy profit.

A realistic Etsy pricing example

Imagine you sell a handmade necklace.

ItemAmount
Selling price$30.00
Shipping charged to customer$5.00
Material cost$8.00
Packaging cost$1.50
Shipping cost you pay$4.50
Estimated Etsy/payment fees$3.60

Your revenue is $35.00 because the customer paid $30.00 for the product and $5.00 for shipping.

Your costs and fees are $17.60.

That leaves an estimated profit of $17.40.

That sounds decent, but there is still one more question: how long did it take to make, package, photograph, list, and support that product? If the product takes a lot of labor time, the profit may not be as strong as it looks.

This is why pricing is not only about covering materials. It is about building a product that can survive fees, shipping, labor, discounts, and future cost increases.

Why handmade sellers underprice themselves

Underpricing usually does not happen because sellers are careless. It happens because they are trying to be fair, competitive, and beginner-friendly. The problem is that a price that feels “safe” can still be financially wrong.

1. Fear of charging too much

Many Etsy sellers worry that a higher price will scare buyers away. That fear is understandable, especially when a shop is new. But if the price is too low, every order becomes a small financial leak. More sales will not fix that. More sales will only create more low-profit work.

2. Copying competitors

Competitor research is useful, but copying prices is dangerous. A competitor may have cheaper suppliers, faster production, lower shipping costs, or a different goal. Some sellers are also underpricing themselves without realizing it.

3. Forgetting labor time

If a product takes 45 minutes to make and the profit is $6, the business may not be sustainable. You do not need to calculate labor perfectly at the beginning, but you should at least ask whether the profit is worth the time.

4. Ignoring packaging

Packaging costs feel small until they happen on every order. A box, mailer, label, tissue paper, sticker, thank-you card, and protective material can quietly remove several dollars from profit.

5. Treating free shipping as free

Free shipping is not free for the seller. If you offer it, the shipping cost still needs to be built into your product price or covered by a healthy margin.

How to know if an Etsy product is worth selling

A product is not automatically worth selling just because it gets orders. A product is worth selling when the numbers still make sense after the sale.

Look for these signs:

  • Positive profit: every order should leave money after costs and fees.
  • Healthy margin: the product should have room for discounts, refunds, and cost changes.
  • Repeatable production: you should be able to make or fulfill it without burning out.
  • Pricing room: the market should allow a price that supports your business.
  • Low dependency on discounts: the product should not require constant sales just to move.

What profit margin should Etsy sellers aim for?

There is no single perfect profit margin for every Etsy shop. The right margin depends on the product type, labor, shipping, competition, and whether the item is handmade, digital, custom, or print-on-demand.

Digital products often have higher margins because there is no physical shipping or material cost after creation. Handmade jewelry may have strong margins if material costs are controlled. Print-on-demand products usually have thinner margins because production costs are higher. Custom products may need higher pricing because they take more communication and labor.

Instead of chasing one universal number, focus on this question:

After fees, costs, shipping, packaging, and labor, is this product still worth selling?

The pricing mistake that keeps Etsy shops small

The most dangerous pricing strategy is pricing only to get sales.

Sales feel good. They create momentum. They make your shop look active. But if every sale is underpriced, growth becomes exhausting. You ship more orders, answer more messages, buy more supplies, and spend more time, but your bank account does not improve enough.

This is how a shop can grow revenue while the owner still feels broke.

A healthier goal is not “How can I get the most sales?” The better goal is “How can I get profitable sales from the right buyers?”

Common Etsy pricing mistakes

  • Forgetting shipping supplies.
  • Treating free shipping as free.
  • Ignoring transaction fees on shipping.
  • Not accounting for refunds or replacements.
  • Not raising prices when costs increase.
  • Using discounts without recalculating profit.
  • Copying competitors without knowing their costs.
  • Forgetting labor time.

FAQ

How do I calculate Etsy profit?

Start with the selling price plus shipping charged, then subtract Etsy fees, product cost, shipping cost, packaging cost, and ad cost. You can do this manually or use CraftProfitCalc to calculate it faster.

What is a good profit margin for Etsy?

A good margin depends on the product. Digital products, handmade goods, stickers, print-on-demand items, and custom products all have different cost structures. The important thing is that your margin leaves enough room for fees, mistakes, discounts, and labor.

Should I offer free shipping on Etsy?

Free shipping can work, but it is not free for you. If you offer free shipping, include the shipping cost in your pricing strategy so it does not erase your profit.

Do Etsy fees include shipping?

When calculating real profit, you should consider both the shipping amount charged to the buyer and the actual shipping cost you pay. Fees and rules can vary, so always check your own settings and numbers.

How much should I charge for handmade items?

Your price should cover materials, packaging, shipping, platform fees, labor, and profit. Do not price handmade products only by copying competitors.

Why am I getting Etsy sales but not making money?

You may be underpricing, forgetting hidden costs, spending too much on shipping or ads, discounting too aggressively, or selling products with weak margins.

Is an Etsy profit calculator better than a spreadsheet?

A calculator is faster for quick pricing checks. A spreadsheet is useful when tracking many products over time. Many sellers use both.

Conclusion

The goal is not just to make more sales. The goal is to build a shop where every sale makes sense.

When you understand your costs, fees, and profit margin, pricing becomes less emotional and more strategic. Before you list your next product, run the numbers. A few minutes of calculation can save you from months of selling products that do not actually support your shop.
